Referral, 20 March 2026. Lakeside Medical Centre, Mississauga, Ontario. To: Neurology Clinic.
Re: Numbness and tingling in both feet, eight months.

Thank you for seeing Margaret Olsen, a 64-year-old retired librarian with a ten-year history of type 2
diabetes. For the past eight months she has noticed gradually worsening numbness and tingling in both
feet, extending up to about the level of her ankles in a stocking-like pattern. She describes an occasional
burning sensation at night but no pain during the day. She has not noticed any change in her hand grip,
her handwriting, or her ability to button clothing, and she has had no falls or tripping.

On examination, sensation to light touch and pinprick was reduced over both feet in a stocking
distribution, with normal sensation above the ankles. Vibration sense was reduced at both great toes.
Ankle reflexes were absent bilaterally; knee reflexes were normal. Strength was normal throughout, 5/5 in
all limb groups, with no wasting or fasciculations. Her gait was normal and she walks without any aid. I
suspect a distal symmetric diabetic polyneuropathy.

Her HbA1c six weeks ago was 8.1%. She takes metformin and has no other significant history.

Dr. Samuel Okafor, MD, FRCPC
Neurologist
