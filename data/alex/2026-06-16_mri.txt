Diagnostic Imaging
MRI brain and cervical spine, with and without contrast
16 June 2026
Alex Chen
Requested by: Dr. H. Weiss-Okafor
Clinical information: progressive upper and lower limb weakness with brisk reflexes. Exclude cord compression. Query motor neuron disease.

A. Brain, axial FLAIR at the level of the internal capsule. Symmetric hyperintensity along the posterior limb of the internal capsule on both sides, following the corticospinal tracts.
B. Brain, axial susceptibility-weighted near the vertex. A thin band of low signal along the precentral gyrus on both sides, sometimes called the motor band sign.
C. Cervical spine, sagittal T2. Normal alignment. No disc protrusion, canal stenosis, cord compression or intrinsic cord signal change from the foramen magnum to T2.

Impression
- Bilateral, symmetric corticospinal tract signal change and a motor band sign. These findings are supportive of a degenerative process affecting the upper motor neurons in the appropriate clinical context. They are not specific.
- No structural lesion of the brain or cervical cord to account for the patient's weakness. Specifically, no cervical spondylotic myelopathy, no demyelinating lesion, no mass.
- No abnormal enhancement after contrast, so nothing to suggest tumour, infection or inflammation.
